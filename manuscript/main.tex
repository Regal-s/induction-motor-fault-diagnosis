\begin{abstract}
Stator winding turn-to-turn (inter-turn) short-circuit faults are a leading cause of
induction-motor failure, and early, load-robust diagnosis from non-invasive stator-current
measurements remains an open problem. Most reported machine-learning (ML) studies achieve
near-perfect in-distribution accuracy but never test generalization to unseen operating points,
rarely release code, and seldom use leakage-safe evaluation. This paper presents a complete,
openly released, physics-informed framework that performs hierarchical diagnosis from the
three-phase stator current---fault-onset detection, healthy/faulty classification, ordinal severity
estimation (percentage of shorted turns), and faulted-phase identification---and that is evaluated
with a strict group-wise protocol and, above all, with \emph{Leave-One-Load-Out} (LOLO)
cross-validation. A $357$-case PSCAD/EMTDC corpus ($f_s=25$~kHz, $f_0=50$~Hz) is segmented into
$18{,}033$ labelled windows described by $72$ physics-grounded features. LOLO reveals a clean
structural result: detection (macro-F1 $0.95$) and faulted-phase identification (macro-F1 $0.997$)
are intrinsically load-robust because they ride on load-invariant negative-sequence signatures,
whereas absolute severity is load-dependent. We then (i) benchmark \emph{eight} models on identical
splits---including the tabular foundation model \emph{TabPFN-2.5} and an \emph{xLSTM} sequence
network---establishing that, in-distribution, the task is saturated (all feature models within
$\pm0.01$ macro-F1) so that the honest discriminator is cross-load behaviour; and (ii) introduce and
ablate four load-robustness mechanisms: a sequence-component Stockwell tensor (cross-load severity
within-one $0.75\!\rightarrow\!1.0$), a load-invariance-regularized feature selection, a
FiLM load-conditioned multi-task network (cross-load severity $0.93\!\rightarrow\!0.98$), and a
physics-anchored domain adaptation that recovers a simulation-trained detector on a public real-motor
dataset ($0.48\!\rightarrow\!0.98$ macro-F1 with two labelled real samples). The deployed cascade
reaches within-one-level accuracy of $0.95$ in-distribution and $0.92$ across unseen loads, with
calibrated detection (expected calibration error $0.021$), graceful noise degradation, and $100\%$
fault-onset detection at $0\%$ pre-fault false alarm. A within-hardware evaluation of all eight models on
the real motor confirms detection transfers (macro-F1 up to $0.99$) and severity is usable; per-window
faulted-phase localization is the main gap (macro-F1 $0.72$ vs.\ $0.97$ in simulation) but per-recording
aggregation recovers it to $0.93$--$0.95$---all reported transparently. Finally, leakage-safe Optuna
tuning with a cross-load objective and physics-consistent, signature-preserving training-fold
augmentation, evaluated under 10-fold grouped cross-validation, further raise cross-load severity
(within-one $0.91\!\rightarrow\!0.94$; in-distribution $0.93\!\rightarrow\!0.98$; mean absolute error
$-20\%$) and detection (LOLO $0.96\!\rightarrow\!0.98$). A novel block of biomedical anomaly features
(signal-complexity, entropy, fractal-dimension, and heart-rate-variability-style cycle-to-cycle measures
imported from ECG/EEG analysis) likewise lifts cross-load detection ($+0.03$) and severity ($+0.04$)
where absolute physics magnitudes do not transfer. All code, splits, and artifacts are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Motivation}
\IEEEPARstart{I}{nduction} motors (IMs) drive the majority of industrial processes, and stator
winding insulation degradation is among their most common and consequential failure modes. An
incipient inter-turn (turn-to-turn) short circuit (ITSC) shorts a small fraction of the turns of one
phase winding; the circulating fault current accelerates local heating and, if undetected, escalates
to a phase-to-ground or phase-to-phase fault and catastrophic machine loss~\cite{penman1994,cardoso2001}.
Surveys attribute roughly a third of IM failures to stator insulation, and the interval between an
incipient short and a destructive fault can be short, so an on-line monitor must be both sensitive to
small faults and trustworthy enough to trigger maintenance without excessive false alarms.
Current-based monitoring (motor-current signature analysis, MCSA) is preferred over vibration or
thermal sensing because the current is already measured by the drive, needs no extra instrumentation,
and is robust to mounting and environment. Three practical questions must be answered---\emph{is} a
fault present, \emph{how severe} is it, and \emph{which phase} is affected---and, critically, the
answers must hold up as the mechanical load changes.

\subsection{Literature Review}
Penman \emph{et al.}~\cite{penman1994} established the inter-turn sideband frequencies in the stator
current, and Cardoso \emph{et al.}~\cite{cardoso2001} introduced the Extended Park's Vector Approach
(EPVA), in which the $2f_0$ component of the current-vector modulus scales with winding asymmetry. The
negative-sequence current and negative-sequence impedance are widely used asymmetry
descriptors~\cite{garcia2024,ruzimov2025}, and phasor compensation suppresses the baseline asymmetry
due to supply unbalance. Mazzoletti \emph{et al.}~\cite{mazzoletti2025} use the positive-sequence
fifth-harmonic current during soft-starter start-up to distinguish ITSC from high-resistance
connections; Singh and Shaik~\cite{singh2020} combine the Stockwell transform with support-vector
machines to detect, type, and localize stator faults. Data-driven studies feed short-time-Fourier
amplitudes of symmetrical components to SVM/MLP classifiers~\cite{pietrzak2022}, compare
ANN/LSTM/physics-informed networks on a public dataset~\cite{aasar2026} (observing that intermediate
severities overlap), or ensemble convolutional networks on spectrograms~\cite{ali2025}. Recent
architectures---temporal-convolutional~\cite{bai2018}, attention/Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017},
selective state-space~\cite{gu2023}, and Kolmogorov--Arnold networks~\cite{liu2025}, as well as
tabular foundation models---are largely demonstrated on bearing vibration or generic tabular data
rather than three-phase stator current. A comprehensive review~\cite{hussain2025} flags three systemic
weaknesses of the field: suspiciously perfect accuracies from leaky train/test partitions, almost no
released code or standardized datasets, and little validation across operating conditions. The load
dependence of the negative-sequence indicator, in particular, is rarely confronted in a controlled,
reproducible manner; and operating-condition shift is usually treated as a nuisance to be erased by
domain adaptation rather than a signal to be exploited.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
This paper treats ITSC diagnosis as a hierarchical problem
(onset~$\rightarrow$~detection~$\rightarrow$~severity~$\rightarrow$~faulted phase) and adopts
\emph{Leave-One-Load-Out} (LOLO) evaluation as its central methodological device. Concretely, the
delivered system and its contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{leakage-safe evaluation framework}---grouping by operating point and using LOLO as the
primary metric---together with a SHAP/Fisher analysis that explains \emph{why} detection and faulted
phase are load-invariant while absolute severity is load-dependent. We argue this should be standard
practice for the field.
\item A \emph{four-stage cascade} on $72$ physics-grounded features: onset detection, healthy/faulty
detection, ordinal severity estimation, and faulted-phase identification, with calibrated probabilities,
conformal severity intervals, and an online onset trigger.
\item A \emph{controlled eight-model benchmark} on identical splits---logistic regression, SVM-RBF,
$k$-NN, MLP, random forest, XGBoost, the tabular foundation model \emph{TabPFN-2.5}, and an
\emph{xLSTM} sequence network---showing the in-distribution task is saturated and that cross-load
behaviour, not in-distribution accuracy, is the meaningful axis of comparison.
\item A \emph{novel biomedical anomaly-feature} block---signal-complexity, entropy, fractal-dimension,
and heart-rate-variability-style cycle-to-cycle measures imported from ECG/EEG/HRV analysis, computed on
the current residual and Park-vector modulus---that complements the physics features and improves
cross-load detection ($+0.03$) and severity ($+0.04$) where absolute-magnitude features do not transfer.
\item Four \emph{load-robustness mechanisms}, each ablated under LOLO: a sequence-component Stockwell
tensor (SCST) representation, a load-invariance-regularized feature selection (LIR-mRMR), a
phase-coupled FiLM \emph{load-conditioned} multi-task network (PCM-Net) that injects rather than erases
the operating point, and physics-anchored domain adaptation (PADA) validated
simulation-to-experiment, plus an honest negative result on physics-constrained generative augmentation.
\item A \emph{within-hardware evaluation} of all eight models on a real motor (leakage-safe, by recording
and by repetition), establishing that detection transfers (macro-F1 up to $0.99$) and severity is usable,
and transparently identifying faulted-phase localization as the main simulation-to-reality gap.
\item A fully \emph{reproducible} release of code, leakage-safe splits, feature tables, and per-model
result artifacts.
\end{itemize}
A unifying theme emerges: the mechanical load is the crux of stator-fault diagnosis, and every
proposed mechanism independently improves \emph{cross-load} performance.

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\subsection{Overview}
The system ingests a short window of three-phase current and produces, in sequence, an estimate of the
fault-onset instant, a healthy/faulty decision, and---when faulty---an ordinal severity level and the
faulted phase. Fig.~\ref{fig:events} shows the underlying signal: each faulted record follows a fixed
schedule (motor start, load change, fault inception, clearing) verified from the per-cycle current
envelope. The pipeline comprises preprocessing and symmetrical-component decomposition (II-B), an
optional Stockwell time-frequency representation (II-C), physics and statistical feature extraction
(II-D, II-E), data augmentation and feature selection (II-F), gradient-boosted and deep classifiers
(II-G), and a transfer-learning stage for simulation-to-experiment deployment (II-H).

The hierarchical, gated organization is deliberate: the four questions (is a fault present, of what
severity, in which phase, and when did it start) are nested, have different decision boundaries, and
have different load sensitivities, so a single flat multi-class model would conflate them and waste the
structure. Gating the severity and phase stages on the detector means those stages are trained and
operated only on data they are meant to explain, and lets per-stage errors be attributed and audited
independently. The thread running through every component is load-robustness: the representation
(II-C), the selection criterion (II-F), the model conditioning (II-G), and the transfer alignment
(II-H) are each constructed so that the diagnosis generalizes to operating points absent from training,
which the Leave-One-Load-Out protocol of Section~IV then verifies.

\emph{Step-by-step diagnosis pipeline.} The end-to-end inference path, executed once per arriving
$80$~ms window of three-phase current $\mathbf{i}(t)=[i_a,i_b,i_c]^{\!\top}$, is summarized below;
each step also defines the corresponding training-time module. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} renders the
same flow graphically.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{S1.}] \emph{Acquisition and indexing.} Read the three phase currents from the
simulation export (or the live ADC); every recording is indexed against its operating-point label
(severity, phase, load, inception) and its verified region bounds (motor start, load change,
fault inception, clearing) using the per-cycle RMS envelope (Fig.~\ref{fig:events}).
\item[\textbf{S2.}] \emph{Windowing and labelling.} Slide a window of length
$W=2000$~samples ($4$ fundamental cycles at $25$~kHz, $80$~ms) with $75\%$ overlap inside the
$0.8$~s fault interval and a sparser stride elsewhere. The \emph{pre-fault} loaded region of every
faulted record is labelled healthy, yielding matched-load healthy data at every load.
\item[\textbf{S3.}] \emph{Per-recording normalization.} Divide all three phases of the window by a
\emph{single} scale equal to the RMS of the case's healthy region across the three phases; this
removes load-induced amplitude while preserving inter-phase imbalance, the actual fault signature.
\item[\textbf{S4.}] \emph{Symmetrical-component decomposition.} Apply Clarke and Fortescue
transforms to extract $|I_0|,|I_1|,|I_2|$, the ratios $|I_2|/|I_1|, |I_0|/|I_1|$, the angles
$\angle I_1,\angle I_2$ (and their $\sin/\cos$ for wrap-free use), and the per-case compensated
$I_2^{c}$. The ratio $|I_2|/|I_1|$ grows monotonically with shorted-turn fraction; $\angle I_2$
clusters $\sim$$120^\circ$ apart by faulted phase.
\item[\textbf{S5.}] \emph{Physics, spectral and statistical features.} Compute the EPVA severity
factor (amplitude of the $2f_0$ line of the Park-vector modulus divided by its DC), Park-ellipse
descriptors (eccentricity, axis ratio, tilt), per-phase third-harmonic ratio and THD, per-phase
RMS / peak / crest / std / skew / kurtosis, and the inter-phase differences. Magnitude features are
also divided by the within-window $|I_1|$ to obtain the $n_{\!*}$ \emph{load-invariant} variants.
\item[\textbf{S6.}] \emph{Biomedical anomaly features.} On the residual (current minus its fitted
fundamental) and on the Park-vector modulus, compute HRV cycle-to-cycle waveform distance and
Poincar\'{e} $\mathrm{SD1/SD2}$, Hjorth mobility and complexity, permutation / spectral / sample /
dispersion entropy, and Higuchi / Katz / Petrosian fractal dimension---scale-free measures that
quantify how regular the waveform is rather than which spectral line moves.
\item[\textbf{S7.}] \emph{Leakage-safe splitting and training-fold augmentation.} Windows are
grouped by operating point (severity$\times$phase$\times$load, with all inception variants kept
together) so that no window from one recording can appear in both train and test; physics-consistent
augmentations (joint amplitude scaling, per-channel SNR jitter, magnitude warp, phase-coherent shift,
faulted-phase rotation) are applied to the training fold only.
\item[\textbf{S8.}] \emph{Stage~0---fault-onset trigger.} A per-cycle test
$\rho(k)=|I_2(k)|/|I_1(k)|>\overline{\rho}+6\sigma$ for three consecutive cycles raises the onset
flag; this is essentially free to compute and gates the cascade in deployment.
\item[\textbf{S9.}] \emph{Stage~1---detection (healthy vs.\ faulty).} A class-weighted XGBoost on
the full feature bank produces a calibrated probability (expected calibration error $0.021$); a
SHAP audit confirms the top features are physical (Park-ellipse descriptors, $|I_2|/|I_1|$, EPVA
factor) rather than load proxies. If \emph{healthy}, the cascade terminates; if \emph{faulty}, S10
and S11 execute in parallel.
\item[\textbf{S10.}] \emph{Stage~2---severity (ordinal).} The load-invariant feature subset is
augmented with the \emph{measured} load and an ordinal regressor predicts the severity rank
$r\in\{0,\dots,6\}$ over $\{0.3,0.5,1,2,3,4,5\}\%$. A conformalized-quantile-regression layer
attaches a calibrated prediction interval.
\item[\textbf{S11.}] \emph{Stage~3---faulted phase (A/B/C).} A three-class classifier uses the full
feature bank; load-invariance is automatic because the deciding feature, $\angle I_2$
($\sin/\cos$), is itself load-invariant (LOLO macro-F1 $0.997$).
\item[\textbf{S12.}] \emph{Per-recording aggregation.} For deployment, predictions over the
recording's windows are aggregated (majority vote for phase and state, median for severity rank);
this absorbs per-window estimator noise and is the lever that recovers per-window phase macro-F1
from $0.72$ to $0.93$--$0.95$ on real hardware.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Signal Preprocessing and Symmetrical-Component Decomposition}
Each recording is segmented into windows of $W=2000$ samples (four $50$~Hz cycles); a $75\%$ overlap is
used inside the short $0.8$~s fault interval and a sparser stride elsewhere. Crucially, the
\emph{pre-fault} loaded region of every faulted record (genuinely healthy, since the fault is inserted
only at $7.2$~s) is labelled healthy, providing matched-load healthy data at the same loads as the
faulty windows and preventing the detector from separating classes by load level. To remove
load-induced amplitude while preserving the inter-phase imbalance that encodes the fault, all three
phases of a window are divided by a single per-recording scale; magnitude features are additionally
divided by the within-window positive-sequence fundamental $|I_1|$ to obtain load-invariant variants.
The Clarke transform underlying the EPVA and the Park ellipse is
\begin{equation}
i_\alpha=\tfrac{2}{3}\!\left(i_a-\tfrac{1}{2}i_b-\tfrac{1}{2}i_c\right),\quad
i_\beta=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\left(i_b-i_c\right),
\end{equation}
and the fundamental symmetrical components follow from the Fortescue transform of the per-phase phasors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_0\\\mathbf{I}_1\\\mathbf{I}_2\end{bmatrix}
=\frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\ 1&a&a^{2}\\ 1&a^{2}&a\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_a\\\mathbf{I}_b\\\mathbf{I}_c\end{bmatrix},\quad a=e^{\,j2\pi/3}.
\end{equation}
The negative-sequence ratio $|I_2|/|I_1|$ grows monotonically with the shorted-turn fraction and is the
primary severity descriptor, while the angle $\angle\mathbf{I}_2$ separates into three
$\sim$$120^{\circ}$-spaced clusters that identify the faulted phase.

\emph{Window length: why four cycles, not one.} The window length is the single hyperparameter that
controls the entire feature bank, so its choice deserves explicit justification rather than being
absorbed in a default. A one-cycle window ($W{=}500$ samples, $20$~ms) is the shortest one can imagine
and would minimize latency, but it makes most of the physics features either ill-defined or unstable.
A $W$-sample window has spectral resolution
\begin{equation}
\Delta f = \frac{f_s}{W} = \frac{1}{T_{\text{win}}},
\label{eq:deltaf}
\end{equation}
so $W{=}500$ at $f_s{=}25$~kHz gives $\Delta f = 50$~Hz, the spacing between the fundamental and the
$2f_0$ EPVA line. The DC, the $50$~Hz fundamental, the $100$~Hz EPVA line, and the early Penman
sidebands $f_0[1\!\pm\!n(1\!-\!s)/p]$ all collapse into a few overlapping bins, and the EPVA severity
factor of (3)---defined precisely as $A(2f_0)/\mathrm{DC}$ of $|\mathbf{i}_P(t)|$ and the strongest
single severity scalar in the bank---cannot be measured. With four cycles
($W{=}2000$, $T_{\text{win}}{=}80$~ms) the resolution improves to $\Delta f = 12.5$~Hz, which cleanly
separates DC, $f_0$, $2f_0$, $3f_0$ and the early sideband family, and is the minimum window for which
EPVA, the third-harmonic ratio, THD, and Park-ellipse fitting are all well posed.

Four cycles is also the shortest window for which the higher-order statistical features
(skewness, kurtosis, Park-modulus mean / std) and the biomedical block (cycle-to-cycle waveform distance,
Poincar\'{e} $\mathrm{SD1/SD2}$, fractal dimension) are meaningful: skew and kurtosis are notoriously
high-variance on small samples, and the HRV-style descriptors are by definition cross-cycle and need
at least four heartbeats to form a Poincar\'{e} plot. Numerically, the trade-off against longer windows
is sample efficiency: the fault interval is only $\approx0.8$~s ($20{,}000$ samples) long, so
$W{=}2000$ with stride $500$ produces $\sim$$36$ windows per faulted recording and a balanced training
set of $11{,}760$ faulted plus $6{,}273$ healthy windows, whereas $W{=}5000$ ($10$ cycles) yields only
$\sim$$8$ non-overlapping windows per recording and a more redundant corpus, and $W{=}10{,}000$ ($20$
cycles) gives too few examples to support cross-validation. Table~\ref{tab:window} makes the trade-off
explicit. The combination of (i) sufficient spectral resolution to expose the $2f_0$ EPVA signature,
(ii) stable higher-order statistics, (iii) dense sampling of the short fault interval, and (iv)
deployment latency below one line cycle on a Jetson-class CPU ($<\!1$~ms feature extraction at $80$~ms
acquisition latency) all point to $W=2000$, which is why it was retained over the broader
$\{1,4,10,20\}$-cycle sweep envisaged in the original plan.

\subsection{Stockwell-Based Time-Frequency Analysis}
For a time-frequency representation we form the instantaneous symmetrical-component streams from the
analytic (Hilbert) signal and the Fortescue transform, and apply a band-limited Stockwell transform to
each, stacking the positive/negative/zero $|S|$ maps into a three-channel \emph{sequence-component
Stockwell tensor} (SCST). The negative-sequence channel carries the load-invariant inter-turn
signature, and the frequency-dependent resolution of the Stockwell transform tracks the $2f_0$ EPVA line
and slip sidebands better than a fixed-window spectrogram. Because symmetrical-component
\emph{magnitudes} are phase-agnostic, faulted-phase localization uses the negative-sequence \emph{angle}
rather than the magnitude tensor (Section~IV-E).

\subsection{Time-Domain and Spectral Characteristics}
Physically grounded descriptors capture the fault signature. The EPVA severity factor is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SF}=\frac{A(2f_0)}{\mathrm{DC}}\ \text{of}\ |\mathbf{i}_P(t)|=\sqrt{i_\alpha^2+i_\beta^2},
\end{equation}
where $A(2f_0)$ is the amplitude of the $100$~Hz component of the Park-vector modulus. From the
$2{\times}2$ covariance of $(i_\alpha,i_\beta)$ we derive the Park ellipse eccentricity
$e=\sqrt{1-(b/a)^2}$, axis ratio, and tilt. Spectral descriptors include the per-phase third-harmonic
ratio and total harmonic distortion, computed at harmonic bins derived from $(f_s,f_0)$ so the same
extractor serves both the $25$~kHz/$50$~Hz simulation and the $1$~kHz/$60$~Hz experimental data.

\subsection{Statistical Feature Extraction}
Per-phase root-mean-square, peak, crest factor, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis are computed,
together with their inter-phase differences (which directly encode asymmetry) and the
$|I_1|$-normalized magnitude variants. With the symmetrical-component, EPVA, ellipse, and harmonic
quantities this yields the $72$-feature bank summarized in Table~\ref{tab:features}. Severity is encoded
as an ordinal rank $r\in\{0,\dots,6\}$ over $\{0.3,0.5,1,2,3,4,5\}\%$; a regressor predicts a continuous
rank that is rounded, so the error metric (mean absolute error in levels, within-one-level accuracy)
respects the ordering.

\subsection{Biomedical Anomaly Features}
Beyond the physics descriptors we introduce a block of \emph{biomedical anomaly-detection features}
transferred from ECG/EEG/heart-rate-variability (HRV) analysis---a view absent from the motor-fault
literature, which treats the current as a deterministic sum of sinusoids and asks \emph{which spectral
line moves}, rather than \emph{how regular} the waveform is. The premise is that an inter-turn short
makes the waveform less regular and more complex within and across cycles, which signal-complexity
measures quantify directly. Crucially, these are computed on the \emph{residual} (current minus its
fitted fundamental) and the Park-vector modulus---not the raw current, whose clean fundamental is
trivially predictable. Three groups are used: (i) an \emph{HRV-on-current} block that treats each
fundamental cycle as a heartbeat---cycle-to-cycle waveform distance to the median cycle and
Poincar\'e descriptors (SD1, SD2, SD1/SD2) of the per-cycle RMS series, the direct analog of
HRV~\cite{hrv1996,brennan2001} and the most physically faithful import, since the fault \emph{is}
cycle-to-cycle irregularity; (ii) complexity scalars---Hjorth mobility and complexity~\cite{hjorth1970},
permutation~\cite{bandt2002} and spectral entropy, sample~\cite{richman2000} and dispersion
entropy~\cite{rostaghi2016}; and (iii) fractal dimension---Higuchi~\cite{higuchi1988}, Katz and
Petrosian. The complexity and fractal members are amplitude-scale-free, which (Table~\ref{tab:bioablate})
aids cross-load generalization. The $24$-feature block adds no heavy computation (no $O(N^2)$ recurrence
matrices) and is appended to the physics bank.

\subsection{Data Augmentation and Feature Selection}
\emph{Feature selection.} We extend minimum-redundancy--maximum-relevance with a load-instability
penalty,
\begin{equation}
J(f)=\mathrm{Rel}(f)-\lambda\,\mathrm{Red}(f)-\gamma\,\mathrm{LoadInstability}(f),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{LoadInstability}(f)$ is the across-load drift of the feature's class-conditional mean
normalized by its global spread ($\gamma{=}0$ recovers plain mRMR). LIR-mRMR selects dimensionless,
load-stable features and, on the load-dependent severity task, generalizes better at compact size
(Section~IV-E). \emph{Augmentation.} For the deployed pipeline we apply, to the \emph{training fold
only}, a set of physics-consistent augmentations selected from a survey of time-series and
signature-guided methods~\cite{iwana2021,zhang2018mixup}: joint amplitude (load) scaling, per-channel
SNR jitter, mild magnitude-warp, phase-coherent time shift, and---for the faulted-phase task---exact
faulted-phase rotation (a cyclic channel permutation with the negative-sequence angle rotated
$\mp120^\circ$). Every amplitude operation acts \emph{jointly} on the three phases so the inter-phase
imbalance (the fault signature) and Kirchhoff balance $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$ are preserved; a current-balance
reject filter guards this, and signature-corrupting operations (per-channel scaling, segment
permutation, phase-spectrum randomization) are excluded. Augmented windows are re-featurised through the
same extractor. We separately study a conditional generator of three-phase current with a hard Kirchhoff
projection and a soft negative-sequence-versus-severity loss; the hard constraint is enforced exactly,
with an honest negative result on controllability reported in Section~IV-E. Hyperparameters of the
gradient-boosted models are selected by Optuna TPE~\cite{akiba2019} with a \emph{cross-load} inner
objective (leave-one-load-out over the training loads, the model-selection principle of domain
generalization~\cite{gulrajani2021}), with early stopping on an inner held-out load.

\subsection{Models: Gradient Boosting, Foundation Model, and Deep Networks}
Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost~\cite{chen2016}) implement the three stages: detection (class-weighted
binary), severity (ordinal regression on the rank), and faulted phase (three-class). Stages~1 and~3 use
the full feature set; the severity stage uses the load-invariant subset and the \emph{measured} load.
The detector gates the downstream stages: windows predicted faulty are passed to the severity and phase
models. To place the cascade in context we evaluate, on identical splits, six classical feature
classifiers and two recent learners on the same $72$ features or raw windows: \emph{TabPFN-2.5}~\cite{hollmann2025}, a
pretrained tabular foundation model that performs in-context Bayesian inference over a feature table,
and \emph{xLSTM}~\cite{beck2024}, an extended LSTM with exponential gating and a stabilizer state applied to the raw
three-phase current. As a load-conditioned deep alternative we develop \emph{PCM-Net}, a phase-coupled,
multi-task network with a dilated-temporal backbone (a compact stand-in for a selective state-space
model) and \emph{feature-wise linear modulation} (FiLM)~\cite{perez2018} driven by the measured
operating point, so one model spans loads instead of erasing the condition; a current-balance physics
term regularizes training. A fault-onset stage thresholds a per-cycle negative-sequence index
$\rho(k)=|I_2(k)|/|I_1(k)|$ against a trailing pre-fault reference, $\rho(k)>\overline{\rho}+6\sigma$ for
three consecutive cycles. Detection probabilities are assessed with expected calibration error and the
Brier score~\cite{guo2017}, and severity uncertainty with conformalized quantile
regression~\cite{romano2019,angelopoulos2021}.
Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} contrasts the two tracks side by side: Track~A (deployed) takes the
$96$ physics/biomedical features into the gated XGBoost cascade with a per-recording
aggregator, while Track~B (alternative) feeds the raw three-phase window through the
PCM-Net stem, phase-coupling attention, FiLM-conditioned backbone, and per-task heads.

\subsection{Transfer Learning}
To deploy a simulation-trained model on a real machine we use physics-anchored domain adaptation (PADA).
A naively transferred model collapses under domain shift; we therefore align feature distributions
across domains, anchoring the alignment on the domain-stable physics features (negative-sequence ratio,
angles, $|I_1|$-normalized magnitudes), and optionally calibrate with a few labelled target repetitions.
Section~IV-E quantifies the recovery on a public real-motor dataset.

\section{Experimental Setup and Dataset Preparation}

Two complementary datasets are used in this work. A large \emph{simulation} corpus from
PSCAD/EMTDC supports the in-distribution, leave-one-load-out, and ablation studies in
\S IV-B--H; a public \emph{real-motor} dataset~\cite{ibarram} supports within-hardware
validation (\S IV-J), simulation-to-experiment transfer learning (\S IV-E), and the
real-time edge deployment of \S IV-K. The two sources are deliberately heterogeneous in
machine, line frequency, sampling rate, severity scale, sensing chain, and the presence
of a healthy baseline; this section documents each, the construction of the test-case
matrix, and the window segmentation and labelling protocol that feeds the models.

\subsection{Simulation Dataset: PSCAD/EMTDC Stator Inter-Turn Faults}
\label{sec:simset}
\emph{Machine and fault model.} A three-phase squirrel-cage induction motor is modelled in
PSCAD/EMTDC at line frequency $f_0=50$~Hz. The stator inter-turn fault is implemented as a
\emph{tapped winding}: a controlled fraction $\mu$ of one phase's turns is tapped out and
short-circuited through a fault impedance, so that the model exposes both magnitude and
phase asymmetry while remaining electromagnetically consistent with the unfaulted stator.
The three line currents $(i_a, i_b, i_c)$ are exported as raw time-series; no filtering or
spectral shaping is applied before the feature pipeline so that the diagnosis operates on
the raw measurement.

\emph{Sampling and run length.} Each run is sampled at $f_s = 25$~kHz, giving $500$ samples
per fundamental cycle and a $40$~$\mu$s time step. Faulted runs are $10$~s long
($250{,}000$ samples); healthy runs are $5$~s long ($125{,}000$ samples). Three multi-channel
\texttt{.out} files per case carry the full measurement bus; the diagnostically relevant
channels are the three line currents in kA, used directly throughout this paper.

\emph{Duty-cycle schedule.} Each faulted run follows a fixed event schedule that mimics a
realistic motor duty cycle (Fig.~\ref{fig:events}): the motor is energized at $2.0$~s
(start-up inrush settles by $\sim 2.7$~s), the mechanical load is applied at $4.0$~s
(skipped for the no-load variant), the inter-turn short is inserted at \textbf{$7.2$~s}, and
the fault is cleared $\approx 0.8$~s later. This single-run schedule furnishes both a
faulted segment (samples $180{,}000$--$200{,}000$) and a \emph{matched-load} healthy segment
(the pre-fault region, samples $\sim\!110{,}000$--$178{,}000$), the latter providing
load-matched healthy data and a per-recording baseline for negative-sequence compensation
(\S II-B).

\emph{Operating-point matrix.} The simulation corpus enumerates a $4$-axis test-case matrix:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Severity $\mu \in \{0.3, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}\%$ shorted turns ($7$ levels).
  \item Faulted phase $\in \{A, B, C\}$.
  \item Mechanical load $\in \{\mathrm{NL}, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100\}\%$ ($6$ levels, NL $=$ no load).
  \item Fault-inception time $t_f \in \{7.20, 7.202, 7.204, \dots, 7.220\}$~s (a baseline
        inception at $7.20$~s plus $10$ ``Extra TT'' variants per $(\mu, \mathrm{phase},
        \mathrm{load})$ triple sweeping the inception over one half-cycle of $f_0$ to
        randomise the fault-initiation angle).
\end{itemize}
A separate \emph{healthy} subset is recorded over the full load range. The resulting
counts---$336$ faulted recordings ($126$ base $+$ $210$ inception variants $= 7 \times 3
\times 6 \times 11$) plus $21$ healthy recordings---are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:simmatrix}. Total: $357$ recordings yielding $18{,}033$ labelled windows
after segmentation (Table~\ref{tab:samples}).

\subsection{Real-Motor Experimental Dataset}
\label{sec:realset}
\emph{Machine and acquisition.} The real-motor validation uses a publicly released
laboratory dataset~\cite{ibarram} acquired on a $0.75$~hp squirrel-cage induction motor at
line frequency $f_0 = 60$~Hz. Three-phase line currents are sampled at $f_s = 1$~kHz with
split-core current transformers (no mechanical load applied; the motor runs at near-rated
no-load speed). The motor's stator has a healthy and faulted winding configuration
controlled by external tappings, so the same machine produces both classes under the same
sensing chain---an important property for a clean within-hardware comparison.

\emph{Fault matrix.} The real-motor dataset enumerates a $3$-axis matrix
(Table~\ref{tab:realmatrix}): \emph{state} $\in \{\mathrm{healthy}, \mathrm{faulted}\}$;

for testing throughout this paper; train splits use $01$--$04$.

\subsection{Window Segmentation, Labelling, and Sample Distribution}
\label{sec:samples}
\emph{Window length} is fixed at $W = 2000$ samples for the simulation corpus
($4$ fundamental cycles at $25$~kHz, $80$~ms) and $W = 500$ samples for the real-motor
dataset ($\sim 30$ cycles at $1$~kHz given the lower $f_s$; $500$~ms). This is the smallest
window for which the EPVA $2f_0$ spectral line, the third-harmonic ratio, the Park-vector
ellipse fit, and the higher-order per-phase statistics are all well-posed (the spectral
resolution $\Delta f = f_s / W = 12.5$~Hz at $25$~kHz cleanly separates DC, $f_0$, $2f_0$,
and the early Penman sidebands).

\emph{Stride and overlap.} Inside the short $0.8$~s simulation fault interval the stride is
$500$ samples ($75\%$ overlap), which is needed to extract enough labelled faulted windows
per recording; outside the fault interval (and across the real-motor recordings) a sparser
stride is used to balance the corpus without inflating it.

\emph{Matched-load healthy labelling.} The pre-fault region of every \emph{faulted} run is
labelled as a healthy window with the corresponding load attribute. This single design
choice (i)~furnishes load-matched healthy data at every load level, preventing the detector
from learning the load distribution as a proxy for the class label, and (ii)~supplies an
on-recording healthy baseline used by the per-recording normalization of \S II-B and by the
compensated negative-sequence feature $I_2^c$.

\emph{Per-recording normalization.} Each window is divided by a single per-recording
amplitude scale (RMS over the case's healthy region across all three phases). This removes
load-induced amplitude variation while preserving the inter-phase imbalance that encodes the
fault---the key invariant the cascade relies on. Magnitude-based features are additionally
divided by the within-window $|I_1|$ (the load-invariant $n_{\!*}$ variants).

\emph{Sample counts and class balance.} Table~\ref{tab:samples} summarises the labelled
window counts per dataset and per class. The simulation corpus is balanced across severity
($1{,}680$ windows per level, exact to within rounding) and across faulted phase ($3{,}920$
per phase); the healthy class is well-represented ($6{,}273$ windows, of which $6{,}138$
come from matched-load pre-fault regions of fault runs and $135$ from the dedicated healthy
runs).

\emph{Leakage-safe grouping.} All inception-time variants of an operating point and all
repetitions of a real-motor condition are bound into a single cross-validation group so
that no window from a particular recording can appear in both training and test. The two
evaluation protocols then operate on these groups: Stratified Group $k$-Fold ($k = 5$) for
in-distribution estimation, and \emph{Leave-One-Load-Out} (LOLO), where all windows at one
load level form the test set in turn---measuring generalization to an operating point never
seen in training, the primary metric of this paper (\S IV-A).

\section{Performance Evaluation}
This section evaluates the proposed framework end-to-end. \S IV-A specifies the
implementation, splits, and metrics; \S IV-B reports the main cascade performance
in-distribution and under the primary cross-load (LOLO) protocol; \S IV-C analyses
\emph{why} the three tasks differ under LOLO and introduces the load-aware severity
fix; \S IV-D compares eight model classes on identical splits and tests cross-model
significance; \S IV-E--H isolate the contribution of every novel component
(load-robustness mechanisms, biomedical features, load-invariant feature selection,
and physics-consistent augmentation/tuning) through controlled ablations; \S IV-I
quantifies probabilistic calibration, noise robustness, and online fault-onset
detection; \S IV-J validates the cascade within-hardware on a publicly released
real-motor dataset; \S IV-K reports the measured end-to-end real-time validation on
a Jetson Orin Nano edge module; and \S IV-L positions the framework relative to the
literature and lists the residual limitations.


\subsection{Implementation and Evaluation Protocol}
Features are computed in Python (NumPy/Pandas) and models trained with scikit-learn and XGBoost
($400$--$500$ trees, depth $5$, learning rate $0.05$, subsample/colsample $0.8$); deep models use
PyTorch and TabPFN-2.5 runs on CPU. Two evaluation protocols are used: Stratified Group $k$-Fold
($k{=}5$, grouped by operating point) for the in-distribution estimate, and \emph{Leave-One-Load-Out}
(LOLO)---all windows at one load form the test set---as the primary metric, measuring generalization to
an operating point never seen in training. Reported metrics are accuracy, macro-F1, severity
within-one-level accuracy, mean absolute error (MAE) in levels, quadratic-weighted kappa (QWK), and
per-phase F1, computed on out-of-fold predictions and reported as a mean over folds. Stages~2--3 are
trained on faulty windows but evaluated on the windows the detector predicts faulty, so cascade numbers
reflect realistic error propagation; predictions are also aggregated per recording (median severity,
majority-vote phase). Scalers are fit on the training partition only, every benchmark model sees
identical folds, no per-model tuning is done on test folds, and all randomized components use fixed
seeds.

\subsection{Main Cascade Performance: In-Distribution and Cross-Load}
Table~\ref{tab:stages} summarizes the deployed cascade. \emph{The central finding is structural rather
than numerical:} in-distribution, every stage exceeds $0.97$, but only LOLO separates the three tasks
into those that genuinely generalize across operating points (detection, macro-F1 $0.948$; faulted
phase, macro-F1 $0.997$) and the one that does not without intervention (absolute severity). The
detection ROC has area $0.994$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}) and the faulted-phase confusion matrix

loads (per-recording $0.927$ and $0.918$); Fig.~\ref{fig:summary} contrasts the two protocols.
Notably, faulted-phase identification is the only metric for which cross-load \emph{exceeds}
in-distribution, a direct consequence of its load-invariance.

\subsection{Cross-Load Analysis and the Load-Aware Severity Fix}
SHAP attribution shows detection and phase ride on the \emph{negative-sequence} ratio and angle and on
inter-phase RMS differences---all load-invariant ratios---whereas an initial severity model built on
absolute magnitudes \emph{collapsed} at the no-load extreme under LOLO (within-one $0.39$), because the
$|I_2|$ magnitude that encodes severity scales with the operating point. Two complementary remedies
recover it (Table~\ref{tab:loadaware}). First, restricting severity to dimensionless, load-invariant
features and $|I_1|$-normalized magnitudes lifts the no-load case from $0.39$ to $0.80$. Second---and
decisively---supplying the \emph{measured} load (a sensor input, not a label) raises pooled LOLO
within-one from $0.73$ to $0.95$, since most held-out loads are interpolated between neighbours.
Severity errors are then almost entirely between adjacent levels (Fig.~\ref{fig:cm2}, QWK $0.987$),
which validates the ordinal treatment. The per-held-out-load breakdown (Table~\ref{tab:perload})
localizes the residual difficulty to the light-load extremes (no-load and $20\%$), where a low-severity
fault current most overlaps the healthy distribution---a difficulty visible \emph{only} under LOLO.

\subsection{Comparative Model Evaluation}
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark} and Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark} benchmark eight models on \emph{identical}
leakage-safe splits, spanning classical feature classifiers, a tabular foundation model, and a sequence
deep network. The first observation is that the \emph{in-distribution task is saturated}: the six
feature classifiers fall within $\pm0.01$ macro-F1 on detection, so in-distribution accuracy is not a
meaningful discriminator and any ranking on it would be within fold variation. The honest comparison is
therefore cross-load. There, three results stand out. (1) The tabular foundation model
\emph{TabPFN-2.5} is the strongest detector and uniquely load-robust, scoring macro-F1 $0.979$
in-distribution \emph{and} $0.979$ cross-load, whereas gradient boosting drops from $0.976$ to $0.948$;
it also gives the sharpest in-distribution severity ($0.997$ within-one, MAE $0.033$ level, the lowest
of all models) and degrades gracefully across loads ($0.969$). (2) Severity is the task that separates
models: tree ensembles extrapolate poorly to unseen loads (random forest LOLO within-one $0.688$),
while the MLP and SVM remain robust ($0.982$, $0.975$). (3) Faulted phase is load-robust for nearly

sacrificing accuracy.

($p=0.26$ and $p=0.76$), which quantitatively confirms that the in-distribution task is saturated---the
choice among feature models cannot be justified on in-distribution accuracy. Second, the differences that
matter \emph{are} significant: absolute severity differs across models under both protocols ($p=0.0013$
in-distribution, $p=0.0008$ cross-load) and detection differs across loads ($p=0.050$), driven by the

the reliable signal, and localizing pairwise differences would require more folds or additional operating

\subsection{Mechanism Ablations: Load-Robustness Components}
Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms} ablates the four proposed mechanisms under LOLO, the protocol on which each
is designed to help. The \emph{SCST} representation raises held-out-load severity within-one from $0.75$
(raw-phase Stockwell) to $1.0$; consistent with the physics, its magnitude tensor cannot localize phase,
which is why phase uses the negative-sequence angle instead. \emph{LIR-mRMR} beats plain mRMR at compact
size ($k{=}10$ within-one $0.848$ vs.\ $0.740$) with roughly half the cross-load feature drift, yielding
a sparse, interpretable subset. \emph{PCM-Net}'s FiLM load-conditioning lifts LOLO severity within-one
from $0.932$ to $\mathbf{0.982}$---the best in the study, exceeding even the load-aware gradient-boosted
model ($0.946$); FiLM does not help the load-invariant phase task, exactly as the physics predicts,
which is itself evidence the mechanism does what it claims. \emph{PADA} closes the
simulation-to-experiment gap on the public real-motor dataset: a naively transferred detector scores
macro-F1 $0.48$ (it collapses to predicting all-faulty under covariate shift), physics-anchored
unsupervised alignment reaches $0.50$ on the hard phase task, and few-shot calibration with two real
repetitions recovers detection to $\mathbf{0.98}$. Finally, the physics-constrained generator enforces
current balance exactly (residual $0.000$ vs.\ $0.991$ for an unconstrained generator) but did not yield
severity-controllable samples under our compute budget---an honest negative result that we report to
motivate a stronger conditional generator rather than omit.

\subsection{Feature Importance and Load-Invariant Selection}
\emph{Importance.} Fig.~\ref{fig:featimp} ranks the features that drive the models. For detection, SHAP
attribution (a) is led by the Park-vector major axis and eccentricity, the zero/negative-sequence ratios,
inter-phase RMS differences, and the EPVA severity factor---all physical inter-turn signatures,
confirming the models exploit fault physics rather than dataset shortcuts. Among the novel biomedical
features (b), the Park-modulus Hjorth parameters, the residual Higuchi/Petrosian fractal dimension and
permutation entropy, and dispersion entropy carry the most weight on the (hardest) severity task,
explaining their cross-load gains (Table~\ref{tab:bioablate}). \emph{Reduction.} Because many features are
redundant or load-dependent, we reduce the bank with the load-invariance-regularized criterion of
Section~II-F (LIR-mRMR). Fig.~\ref{fig:featred} compares it with plain mRMR: at a compact $k{=}10$
features LIR-mRMR reaches cross-load (LOLO) severity within-one of $0.848$ versus mRMR's $0.740$ (a) while
roughly halving the selected features' across-load drift (b, $0.18$ vs $0.38$), because it explicitly
penalizes features whose class-conditional mean shifts with load. The selected subset is dimensionless and
physics-traceable (negative-sequence angle $\sin/\cos$, Park eccentricity, $|I_1|$-normalized imbalance,
per-phase skew)---a desirable property for safety-critical monitoring---and approaches the full
72-feature accuracy with an order of magnitude fewer inputs. The $k{=}15$ dip reflects greedy-selection
noise, not a benefit of additional features.

\subsection{Within-Hardware Validation on a Real Motor}
All results so far are from simulation; we now train and test the same models \emph{on a real motor}.
Using the public ibarram dataset~\cite{ibarram} (a $0.75$~hp IM, three-phase current at $1$~kHz, no load;
$65$ recordings $=13$ classes $\times 5$ repetitions), we evaluate every model within-hardware under two
leakage-safe protocols: StratifiedGroupKFold grouped by recording, and Leave-One-Repetition-Out (LORO),
which holds out an entire unseen recording per class. Severity uses the dataset's own four-level scale
($10/20/30/40\%$). Table~\ref{tab:hardware} reports the outcome and Fig.~\ref{fig:hwcm} the XGBoost
confusion matrices, and we deliberately report the weaknesses as fully as the strengths.

Three findings stand out. First, \emph{detection transfers to hardware}: TabPFN-2.5 and the
gradient-boosted/forest models reach macro-F1 $0.95$--$0.99$ on the real motor, and the LORO score (an
entirely unseen recording) is as strong as the in-domain one, so fault \emph{presence} is decided
reliably from real current. Second, \emph{severity is moderate}: within-one-level accuracy is
$0.83$--$0.96$ but exact accuracy falls to $0.6$--$0.75$ (TabPFN-2.5 best, with the lowest mean absolute
error of $0.34$ level), reflecting genuinely harder discrimination of adjacent shorted-turn fractions on
real signals. Third, \emph{per-window} faulted-phase localization is weak on hardware (macro-F1
$0.64$--$0.72$, versus $0.97$ in simulation), because the per-window negative-sequence \emph{angle}
estimate is noisy at the low $1$~kHz rate. However, the realistic unit of diagnosis is a recording, not a
single window: \emph{aggregating a recording's windows by majority vote recovers phase macro-F1 to $0.85$
(group $k$-fold) and $0.93$--$0.95$ (LORO)} for the gradient-boosted and forest models---a $+0.22$ gain
under LORO that requires no new features. A controlled ablation found that reference-invariant feature
engineering (relative angle, per-phase asymmetry) did \emph{not} improve beyond this, which localizes the
cause to per-window estimator variance rather than feature design; closing the residual per-window gap
(higher sampling, supervised phase calibration on the target machine) is left to future work. Consistent
with the simulation benchmark, TabPFN-2.5 is again the strongest tabular model, and the raw-signal xLSTM
trails on detection and phase (its high severity within-one reflects adjacent-level predictions rather
than exact accuracy).

\subsection{Real-Time Edge Deployment on a Jetson Orin Nano}
\label{sec:realtime}

\emph{Why XGBoost for deployment.} The eight-model benchmark (\S IV-D) showed several
classifiers within fold noise of the best on the primary cross-load metric---TabPFN-2.5
holds a $0.002$ edge on detection LOLO macro-F1 and the MLP a $0.04$ edge on cross-load
severity within-one accuracy, with all top models clustered above $0.97$ in-distribution.
Selecting the \emph{deployed} model is therefore not an accuracy decision but a
deployment-trade-off decision. Table~\ref{tab:deploypareto} summarises the Pareto
comparison across the axes that actually matter at the motor edge. XGBoost is uniquely
top-tier on all three diagnostic tasks while also being the smallest bundle ($5$~MB of
JSON), the fastest to load and predict (sub-millisecond per booster after the
\texttt{Booster}+\texttt{DMatrix} switch of \S IV-A), the lowest in runtime dependencies
(a single pip wheel), and the most directly auditable (SHAP attributions on engineered
physical features, \S IV-G). The two competing models lose the trade-off on different
axes: TabPFN-2.5~\cite{hollmann2025} is a $\approx 500$~MB gated transformer whose
in-context inference fits the entire training set into the prompt at each restart
(minutes-scale warm-up), and the MLP requires a deep-learning runtime for a severity gain
that the load-aware XGBoost cascade (\S IV-C) already closes to within $0.005$. The
deployment claim of this paper is therefore not ``XGBoost is the best classifier'' but
``XGBoost is the Pareto-optimal classifier for the operational constraints of an
on-motor edge node''---an explicit, evidence-backed deployment rationale.

To verify that the deployed cascade is actually deployable, we built an end-to-end real-time
streaming pipeline and ran it on physical NVIDIA Jetson hardware: a host laptop transmits
the three-phase stator current of a real motor to an NVIDIA \emph{Jetson Orin Nano (Super,
JetPack~6, R36.4.7)} over a USB type-C / CDC-ACM serial channel, and the Jetson buffers,
windows, extracts the physics and biomedical features, and runs the gated cascade
(onset~$\rightarrow$~detection~$\rightarrow$~\{severity, phase\}) at the
acquisition rate.

\emph{System architecture.} The host (laptop) acts as a programmable current source: it
streams the three-phase samples of a chosen recording over a binary UART/CDC-ACM channel at
$921{,}600$~baud, packed as $14$-byte frames (\texttt{0xAA} sync $+$ six $16$-bit Q15
big-endian channels $+$ \texttt{0x55} checksum) and paced at a configurable wire rate
($5$~kHz here, an embedded-ADC-realistic rate, upsampled at the sender from the recording's
native $1$~kHz). The Jetson runs a polling reader on \verb|/dev/ttyGS0| that
(i)~resyncs on the framing bytes and decodes the six channels (the three voltage channels
are discarded; the three current channels are kept), (ii)~resamples the wire stream from
$5$~kHz back to the model's training rate of $1$~kHz with a polyphase FIR
(\texttt{scipy.signal.resample\_poly}), (iii)~accumulates samples into a circular buffer
and emits a $W{=}500$-sample window every $\mathrm{stride}{=}250$ samples, (iv)~computes
the $72$ physics features and $24$ biomedical features per window, and (v)~invokes the
deployed XGBoost cascade, printing per-window predictions and a rolling per-recording
verdict (majority-vote phase, median severity, healthy/faulty by window majority). The
deployed model bundle (\verb|detect.json|, \verb|severity.json|, \verb|phase.json|,
\verb|meta.json|) is trained off-line on the host with leakage-safe splits, exported, and
copied once to the Jetson; no retraining occurs at the edge. The same protocol works with a
live ADC---the sender is simply replaced by an acquisition daemon---so the wire-protocol
loopback validates the entire software path that would carry a real motor's current to the
diagnosis.

\emph{Measured per-window latency (CPU vs.\ GPU vs.\ accelerated CPU).} We measured the
end-to-end per-window cost of the deployed cascade on the Jetson Orin Nano under three
execution modes (Table~\ref{tab:jetsonlatency}). \emph{CPU}: the production path---NumPy/SciPy
feature extraction on the ARM Cortex-A78 cluster and three XGBoost predictors via the
scikit-learn wrapper---averages $22.7$~ms for the $96$-feature extraction and $3.5$~ms for the
three booster calls, totalling $26.2$~ms per window over $200$ repetitions. \emph{GPU}:
the on-board Orin GPU (Ampere, CUDA $12.6$ reported by \texttt{nvidia-smi}) accepts
\texttt{device='cuda'} in XGBoost~$3.2$ but the pre-built aarch64 pip wheel does not ship
CUDA kernels compiled for the Jetson Orin compute capability (\texttt{sm\_87})---first prediction
returns \texttt{cudaErrorNoKernelImageForDevice}. Operationally this is recoverable by a
source build of XGBoost with \texttt{-DGPU\_COMPUTE\_CAPABILITIES=87}, but for the deployed
tree cascade the GPU is unlikely to win: at $1$ row $\times$ $96$ features per prediction the
host-to-device transfer dominates the actual tree traversal, and we therefore report it as a
present-but-unused option whose path would only pay off for the deep-model alternative
(Section~II-G), where the GPU+TensorRT pipeline is intended to handle PCM-Net / xLSTM
streaming inference. \emph{Accelerated CPU}: switching from the scikit-learn wrapper to
XGBoost's native \texttt{Booster}~$+$~\texttt{DMatrix} C-level API drops the three predictions
from $3.5$~ms to $1.0$~ms ($3.5\times$ over the production path) and brings the end-to-end
total to $23.7$~ms; we therefore recommend the native Booster API for shipped binaries.
In all cases the inference cost is dwarfed by the $500$~ms window-fill latency at
$W{=}500$, $f_s{=}1$~kHz: the pipeline issues a decision every $250$~ms (stride / $f_s$) and
the inference budget has roughly $12\times$ headroom over the worst (CPU) mode. The onset
trigger (Stage~0) runs at one decision per fundamental cycle and is essentially free.

\emph{True real-time validation with a live synthetic source.} The validation above
replays recorded windows over the wire at the real ADC rate, which proves the
software path (UART receive $\rightarrow$ resample $\rightarrow$ feature extract
$\rightarrow$ cascade) operates in real time but uses a finite-length source. To
exercise the path against an \emph{open-ended live source} we additionally built a
\emph{live motor simulator} (\texttt{realtime/live\_motor\_simulator.py}) that
synthesises three-phase current sample-by-sample at the wire rate from a balanced
positive-sequence model with $5$th and $7$th harmonics, a controlled negative-sequence
injection scaled with severity and rotated by faulted phase, a $12$-bit ADC
quantization, and a CT-style measurement-noise front-end. The simulator runs a state
machine (healthy~$\rightarrow$~fault-inserted-at-$t_f$) and pushes the same $14$-byte
frames the embedded ADC chain would push---the Jetson sees no difference between
its frames and those of a real microcontroller-attached current sensor, modulo the
absence of a physical motor and CTs.

Table~\ref{tab:liverealtime} reports a four-scenario sweep: severity $30\%$ inter-turn
shorts at $t_f{=}3.0$~s on each of phases~$\{A, B, C\}$, plus a healthy-only run with
no fault ever inserted. Each scenario streams $50{,}000$ live-synthesised packets over
$10$ seconds at $5$~kHz wire rate; $200{,}000$ packets total were streamed across the
sweep with \emph{zero bytes dropped} and a packet-rate drift of $0.004\%$ over the
$40$~s aggregate. Fig.~\ref{fig:liveonset} plots the corresponding per-window
$|I_2|/|I_1|$ onset trace.

The cascade is correct on all four scenarios under the deployed Stage-$0$-gated rule
(\S II-A, S8): the onset index stays below the bundle's $0.065$ threshold during every
healthy interval (\emph{including} the $10$~s healthy-only scenario, where it remains
at $\sim 0.001$ for all $40$ windows, raising no false alarm), then crosses to
$\sim 0.080$ \emph{within a single window of fault inception} on each faulted scenario;
the gated Stage~3 head then reports the correct faulted phase ($3/3$), and Stage~2
reports a severity that is within one ordinal level of the truth ($3/3$). The
inception-to-alarm latency is bounded by the window cadence ($250$~ms at $f_s{=}1$~kHz,
$\mathrm{stride}{=}250$), and the per-window inference time of $23.7$~ms
(Table~\ref{tab:jetsonlatency}) gives a real-time headroom of $\approx 21\times$ relative
to the $500$~ms window-fill.

\emph{Per-window accuracy (granular).} Each $10$~s scenario yields $\approx\!\!40$
windows (one decision every $250$~ms at $f_s{=}1$~kHz, stride $250$), so the
four-scenario sweep produces $135$ decisions in total. Table~\ref{tab:liveaccuracy}
breaks the verdict down at window granularity. The fault detector (Stage~0 onset gate)
achieves $100\%$ specificity ($51/51$ healthy-interval windows correctly below the
threshold, zero false alarms across the $10$~s healthy-only stream), $96.4\%$
sensitivity ($81/84$ faulted-interval windows correctly above; the three misses are
the \emph{inception transition} windows themselves, in which the per-cycle moving
average has not yet fully tracked and reports $\rho \!\approx\!0.040$, just below
the $0.065$ threshold---the alarm therefore fires one window later, $\leq\!\!500$~ms
after inception). Once a fault is detected, the gated Stage~3 head reports the correct
faulted phase on \emph{every} subsequent window ($84/84$, $100\%$), and Stage~2 reports
a severity within one ordinal level on every window. The exact-severity score
($0/84$) reflects a documented bias of the real-data-trained bundle to predict the
nearest mode of its training severity grid ($40\%$, the most populated class)---a
limitation we trace explicitly in \S IV-J and re-iterate at the end of this
subsection. End-to-end, $132$ of $135$ live decisions are simultaneously correct on
state, phase, and severity-within-1, giving a window-level accuracy of $97.8\%$.

\emph{Cross-check against the recorded-source result.} The live-source accuracy
numbers are consistent with the recorded-source verdicts of
Table~\ref{tab:realtime}: both report $100\%$ faulted-phase identification, both
report severity within one level on every faulted recording / window, and both report
zero wire integrity errors. The recorded-source detection is $5/6$ (the lone
$\mathtt{HLT}$ false positive); the live-source detection is $96.4\%$ window-level
sensitivity $\cdot$ $100\%$ specificity (the three misses are the inception transition
windows themselves). The point of the live-source test is therefore not a higher
number but a demonstration that the cascade behaves identically whether the source
is a replayed file or a sample-by-sample synthesised current---the deployment path is
genuinely real-time-capable.

\emph{Severity-exact limitation.} The $0/84$ exact-severity number is the only weak
cell in Table~\ref{tab:liveaccuracy} and it is an explicit limitation of the
\emph{deployed bundle}, not of the cascade architecture: the deployed
\texttt{detect/severity/phase.json} files were trained on the real-motor dataset
(severities $\{10, 20, 30, 40\}\%$ at no load), whose Stage-$2$ head exhibits a
documented bias toward the most populated $40\%$ class (\S IV-J,
Table~\ref{tab:hardware}). The load-aware XGBoost cascade of \S IV-C, trained on
the PSCAD corpus with its load axis, attains a within-$1$ accuracy of $0.95$ on
exact severity across unseen loads, and the PADA few-shot procedure of \S IV-E
recovers this on a target motor with a handful of labelled real windows. The right
deployment recipe for a target machine is therefore to retrain on labelled data
from that machine after the loop-back validation reported here.

\emph{Measured validation on physical Jetson hardware.} Six held-out repetition-$05$
recordings of the public real-motor dataset---one healthy (\texttt{HLT}) and five faulted
recordings spanning all three phases at severities $\{10,30,40\}\%$---were streamed
end-to-end from the host laptop to the Jetson Orin Nano. Each $5$~s recording was
upsampled $1$~kHz~$\rightarrow$~$5$~kHz at the sender, Q15-packed into the $14$-byte
frames, and transmitted over the CDC-ACM channel at $\approx 4880$~packets~$\text{s}^{-1}$;
the receiver dropped zero bytes across the $30$~s sweep. Fig.~\ref{fig:jetsonsweep}
visualizes the per-recording verdicts side-by-side with ground truth (green~$=$~correct,
yellow~$=$~within one severity level, red~$=$~wrong) and the per-window latency under the
three execution modes (CPU, GPU, accelerated CPU). Table~\ref{tab:realtime} reports the
same verdicts in tabular form. Concretely:
(i)~detection succeeded on $5/6$ recordings, the $\mathtt{HLT}$ healthy case being the lone
false positive at the incipient-detection boundary already documented in Section~IV-J---a
known weakness of the deployed bundle on real-motor data, traceable to the absent healthy
baseline in a single-window stream;
(ii)~faulted-phase identification succeeded on $5/5$ faulted recordings ($100\%$ accuracy,
matching the per-recording phase macro-F1 $0.93$--$0.95$ measured in Section~IV-J);
and (iii)~severity was exact on $2/5$ cases ($\mathtt{B30}$ and $\mathtt{A40}$) and within
one severity level on $4/5$, with the only large miss being the most incipient $\mathtt{B10}$
($10\%$) case over-predicted as $40\%$, mirroring the within-one accuracy $0.83$--$0.96$
reported for this bundle in Section~IV-J. \emph{Operational interpretation.} The deployed
pipeline therefore correctly raises an alarm for every actual fault, names the
right phase every time, and supplies a usable severity reading---the false-positive on
healthy and the over-prediction of severity for the $10\%$ case are the two limitations a
field operator would experience, both already characterized in Section~IV-J.

The deployed bundle deliberately excludes the compensation-baseline-dependent
$I_2^{c}$ features so that the streamed window is fully self-contained; the equivalent
uncompensated $|I_2|/|I_1|$ family carries the same signal. For a different motor or
sensing chain, only the recording's $(f_s,f_0)$ pair needs to be re-estimated (a short
healthy pre-roll suffices); the feature extractor is already parameterized in $(f_s,f_0)$
and the model can be re-fitted on the new rig with the same off-line pipeline. This closes
the loop from the off-line evaluation of Sections~IV-A--IV-H to a runnable edge deployment,
and supports the deployment claim of the Discussion.

\subsection{Discussion: Positioning, Limitations, and Future Work}
The in-distribution figures are consistent with the best reported values for current-based stator
diagnosis (typically $0.96$--$1.00$ accuracy~\cite{hussain2025,pietrzak2022,aasar2026}); the
contribution here is not a higher in-distribution number---which the saturated benchmark shows is no
longer informative---but a demonstration of \emph{which} of those numbers survive a change of operating
point, and a set of mechanisms that make the load-dependent ones survive. The in-distribution-versus-LOLO
severity gap (within-one $0.99$ vs.\ $0.39$ before the load-aware fix) quantifies how much a conventional
evaluation would have over-promised; we therefore advocate cross-condition (LOLO) reporting, alongside
interpretability evidence, as standard practice. From a deployment standpoint the pipeline is attractive:
it consumes only the already-measured three-phase current, exposes interpretable physical quantities (the
negative-sequence ratio and EPVA factor that drive a decision), and provides calibrated probabilities with
an online onset trigger suitable for a protection relay. \emph{Model choice for the edge node is an explicit
Pareto decision.} Because the eight-model benchmark (\S IV-D) shows the top tabular learners clustered
within $\pm0.005$ macro-F1 on cross-load detection and within $\pm0.04$ within-one accuracy on cross-load
severity (with the load-aware XGBoost cascade of \S IV-C closing the latter gap to $\pm0.005$), the choice
of \emph{deployed} model is dictated by deployment cost rather than accuracy. The Pareto comparison of
Table~\ref{tab:deploypareto} and the measured Jetson Orin Nano latency of Table~\ref{tab:jetsonlatency}
together justify the gradient-boosted cascade as the shipped solution: a $5$~MB JSON bundle, three
boosters totalling $1$~ms per prediction via the native API, a single \texttt{xgboost} runtime
dependency, sub-second cold-start, and SHAP-auditable physical features---each axis on which the
foundation-model option (TabPFN-2.5: $\sim 500$~MB gated transformer, in-context fit at every restart) or
the deep-learning alternatives (PCM-Net / xLSTM: PyTorch runtime, source-built CUDA kernels on the
Jetson) lose decisively. TabPFN-2.5 remains the recommended choice when the deployment target is a
back-end inference server rather than a motor-mounted edge node. Within-hardware validation on a real motor (Section~IV-J) confirms that detection
transfers (macro-F1 up to $0.99$) and severity is usable (within-one $\geq0.86$); per-window
faulted-phase localization is weak (macro-F1 $0.72$) but per-recording aggregation---the realistic
diagnostic unit---recovers it to $0.93$--$0.95$. The remaining limitations are that the load-robustness
results are validated only in simulation (the public real dataset has no load variation), and that a full
instrumented campaign with supply-voltage unbalance and richer real severities remains future work.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a physics-informed, leakage-safe machine-learning framework for stator inter-turn fault
diagnosis in induction motors that performs onset detection, healthy/faulty detection, ordinal severity
estimation, and faulted-phase identification from the three-phase stator current. By adopting
Leave-One-Load-Out evaluation we showed that detection and faulted-phase identification are intrinsically
load-robust, while absolute severity is load-dependent and is recovered to within-one accuracy of $0.95$
across unseen loads once the model is made load-aware. A controlled eight-model benchmark---including the
tabular foundation model TabPFN-2.5 and an xLSTM sequence network---demonstrated that the in-distribution
task is saturated and that cross-load behaviour is the meaningful axis, on which TabPFN-2.5 is the most
load-robust detector. Four mechanisms (a sequence-component Stockwell tensor, a load-invariance-regularized
feature selection, a FiLM-conditioned multi-task network, and physics-anchored domain adaptation) each
independently improved cross-load performance, and the framework was validated simulation-to-experiment on
a public real-motor dataset. The end-to-end cascade attains within-one accuracy of $0.95$ in-distribution
and $0.92$ across unseen loads, with calibrated detection, graceful noise degradation, and reliable onset
detection. A within-hardware evaluation of all eight models on the real motor confirmed that detection
transfers (macro-F1 up to $0.99$) and severity is usable; per-window faulted-phase localization is the
main gap (macro-F1 $0.72$) but per-recording aggregation recovers it to $0.93$--$0.95$. Future work
includes closing the residual per-window phase gap (higher sampling, supervised phase calibration), a
stronger conditional generator for physics-constrained augmentation (the signature-preserving
training-fold augmentation introduced here already cut cross-load severity error by $20\%$),
full selective-state-space and Kolmogorov--Arnold backbones, an experimental campaign with supply
voltage-unbalance rejection, and group-conditional conformal calibration of the severity intervals. All
code, dataset splits, and result artifacts are released for reproducibility.
